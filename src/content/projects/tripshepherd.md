## The work

**Usability testing at volume.** I ran more than ten moderated usability sessions across
the platform, which is where most of the findings came from. Recruiting and moderating
that many sessions in a four-month internship meant treating scheduling as part of the
research design rather than an afterthought.

**Map versus Reels: an A/B test.** The open question was how customers should browse
tours — a map-first interface or a short-form vertical feed. Rather than argue it from
taste, I ran an A/B test and measured both **SUS scores and business KPIs** against each
other.

Tying a usability instrument to commercial metrics is the part I would repeat. A design
can win on SUS and lose on conversion; knowing which one moved, and by how much, is what
turned the decision from a preference into a finding.

**Event-creation flow redesign.** I rebuilt the flow operators use to create an event,
working from the friction the sessions had surfaced.

**Super-app analysis.** A competitive study of super-app patterns, including Mafengwo,
looking at how travel platforms in other markets combine content, community and booking
in one surface.

## Presenting it

I presented the research and the redesign directly to the CEO and CMO. Presenting to
people who can act on findings immediately changes how you write them: the analysis has
to arrive with a recommendation attached, and the recommendation has to survive being
asked *and what would that cost us*.
