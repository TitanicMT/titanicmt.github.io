## C.W. Marine & Powersports

An outdoor activities and equipment rental company in Keswick, Ontario, near Lake
Simcoe — boat and yacht rentals, fishing equipment, and facilitated activities like
ice hut rentals for ice fishing.

The site had a solid booking system and very little else. It was structurally flat,
which made its purpose easy to read: convert online interest into offline bookings.
Its audience is groups and families across a wide age range, arriving from marketing
or word of mouth.

**What I recommended, and why:**

- **Hierarchical categorisation** to group services into broader categories, so the
  user's mental model of the offering stays simple rather than mirroring the company's
  internal list.
- **More descriptive, consistent labelling** in user-centred language, with standardised
  label behaviour so navigation stays predictable.
- **Breadcrumbs and cross-linking** — breadcrumbs to give a sense of location in a site
  that offered none, cross-links between related services to keep a journey moving.
- **Search with metadata and filters**, since a flat structure makes browsing viable but
  finding a specific service slow.
- **Alt text throughout**, which serves accessibility and SEO at once, plus testimonials
  and reviews to give the pages something to say beyond the booking form.

## Comparative critique: four coffee retailers

A structural comparison of Gimme Coffee, Hale Coffee, Bean Wise and Javanut. All four
use topical organisation schemes and top-down structures, but resolve them very
differently:

**Gimme Coffee** runs hierarchical top navigation — Shop, Learn, Visit, Services. Clear
labelling, logical grouping, navigation that matches how users already think.

**Hale Coffee** hides everything behind a hamburger menu. Clean, and a textbook case of
progressive disclosure, but it depends on users recognising the affordance — a
discoverability risk for less experienced visitors.

**Bean Wise** uses a dynamic sidebar with many labels. Good orientation, but the sheer
volume of links tips into information overload: breadth bought at the cost of clarity.

**Javanut** organises horizontally into Coffee, Local Products and Accessories, but has
no Learn or Story section, missing the chance to connect with customers beyond the
transaction.

## Why this work matters

Content audits are the least glamorous thing in this portfolio. They are also the reason
the rest of it functions — every interface decision downstream inherits whatever
structure this stage settles.
